## Crown And Body Detection

- Crown continues to use `_find_meniscus_arc()` and fits the strongest negative vertical-gradient boundary.
- Body now uses `_find_body_brightness_curve()` and follows one fixed boundary definition:
  1. Smooth the grayscale image vertically and horizontally.
  2. Calculate the maximum brightness in each valid column.
  3. Set the column threshold to `maximum - offset`.
  4. Scan from the bottom of the search range upward.
  5. Accept the first lower boundary with at least three consecutive pixels above the threshold.
  6. Interpolate the threshold crossing to a subpixel Y position.
  7. Fit the collected points with the existing robust quadratic curve fitter.
- Invalid columns are discarded instead of being filled with the search-window boundary.
- Scanning from bottom to top keeps Body on the lower bright-band edge, matching the edge polarity used by the Crown maximum-negative-gradient detector.

## Stereo Geometry Findings

- Computed the local projection Jacobian near the current Body point.
- The local epipolar residual is dominated by the image X coordinates, approximately by the difference between `u1` and `u2`.
- Image Y disparity has much greater influence on reconstructed world Z:

```text
dZ ~= 0.011 du1 + 0.770 dv1 - 0.014 du2 - 0.778 dv2 mm
```

- A one-pixel Y change can therefore move reconstructed Z by about `0.77 mm` while changing reprojection error very little.
- Documented that low reprojection error is a necessary geometric consistency check, not proof that two image points represent the same physical feature.
